# Vitni: zero-budget growth roadmap

**Prepared:** 28 October 2026
**Horizon:** 90 days
**Cash budget:** €0
**Working assumption:** one founder can spend about 4–5 hours a week on promotion. These are experiments and output goals, not a promise of reach.

## The growth idea

Lead with the useful outcome: **“See what to try before you replace it.”** Vitni turns a photo and a short description into a practical repair route, pairs it with illustrated guides, and can point people in Paris toward community repair help. Mention AI as the method when relevant; make the repair easier to understand the headline.

Start with people who already have a small repair problem: bike riders, renters and homeowners, and people who want to mend clothes. Treat repair cafés, bike co-ops, makerspaces, and local ateliers as distribution partners as well as a community audience. Keep partner outreach separate from consumer posts.

The repeatable loop is:

```text
Real repair question → useful short video + guide → visit vitni.cc → try a guide
         ↑                                                    ↓
new guide or story ← permission-based outcome feedback ← share the useful link
```

Every post should solve one recognizable problem and point to one next action: **try the related guide** or **show Vitni the problem**. Keep local help as a second route for repairs that need hands-on support.

## What Vitni already has to distribute

- English and French pages, plus a searchable repair guide library.
- Photo-and-description repair requests with illustrated steps.
- A Paris community-help feature and a partner contact page.
- GA4 with consent and privacy limits: it records coarse journey events, not repair descriptions, photos, filenames, or search terms.
- An existing 18-second vertical promo for Reels, TikTok, and Shorts, plus a 110-second pitch video for conversations and partner meetings.

Use the **18-second video as a pinned introduction**, not as the only social content. Its current story already covers diagnosis, a guide, PWA, library, and Paris help. The 110-second film is for a live presentation; it is too long for a discovery post.

### A manageable weekly rhythm (4–5 hours)

- **60 minutes:** choose one real repair question; check it against Search Console, TikTok search suggestions, and questions people actually ask.
- **90 minutes:** record one phone-first demo; make captions readable without sound. Use your own screen recording and repair materials rather than AI-generated footage of Vitni’s UI.
- **45 minutes:** cut one vertical video and one carousel from the same repair. Post natively on the three short-video platforms; avoid simply reposting someone else’s content.
- **45 minutes:** answer relevant community questions and reply to every comment on Vitni’s own posts.
- **45 minutes:** send five individual, useful partner notes or follow-ups. Do not bulk-message.
- **30 minutes:** review the numbers and choose one thing to repeat or change next week.

## Video format to repeat

Use the existing 18-second promo as the brand introduction. For ongoing discovery, make a **20–30 second, one-problem demo**:

| Time | Shot / on-screen words |
|---|---|
| 0–2 sec | Close-up of the problem. Hook: **“Bike chain slipping again?”** |
| 2–6 sec | Show the symptom and say what changed. Keep the real object visible. |
| 6–10 sec | Show the Vitni photo + description flow. Do not reveal personal photos or details. |
| 10–20 sec | Show 2–3 useful illustrated steps, including the safety check or when to stop. |
| 20–25 sec | Show the repaired result only if you actually tested it; otherwise close on the next check. |
| 25–30 sec | **“Try the guide at vitni.cc”** or **“Show Vitni what changed.”** |

Shoot vertically, use a clear first frame, burn in accurate captions, and keep text away from the edges and platform controls. Let the explanation work with sound off. Use the current product screens for UI; the step illustrations can be labeled as illustrations. Never present a generated repair image as proof that a repair worked.

### First three video topics

1. **“My bike chain slips when I change gear.”** Show the chain symptom, Vitni’s likely route, and a check before riding.
2. **“Can this small tear be mended?”** Show a denim seam guide and the materials to check before starting.
3. **“The kitchen sink drains slowly.”** Show a safe, non-invasive first check and a clear stop point before plumbing work becomes uncertain.

### Carousel: “Before replacing a slipping bike chain”

1. **Before replacing it, check why it slips.**
2. **Look:** is the chain still seated on the chainring and cassette?
3. **Notice:** does it slip only when changing gear, or under load too?
4. **Pause:** don’t ride if the chain, wheel, or drivetrain is damaged or unstable.
5. **Follow a clear route:** open Vitni’s illustrated guide for a slipped chain.
6. **CTA:** “Save this for the next ride. Try the guide at vitni.cc.”

Keep the safety wording aligned with the actual guide. Add a source or expert review when the advice depends on a specific component or model.

## Growth experiments: small and measurable

Run one change at a time for at least a week; keep the repair example and posting effort similar when comparing hooks.

| Experiment | Variants | Decision signal |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | “Before you replace it…” vs. “Here’s why it keeps slipping…” | Watch-through, shares/saves, and profile/link visits—not views alone. |
| CTA | “Try the guide” vs. “Show Vitni what changed” | Which brings more guide opens or completed repair requests per landing visit? |
| Category | Bike vs. clothing vs. household fix | Which topic earns useful questions and activated visits, not just likes? |
| Language | English vs. French versions of the same repair | Compare qualified visits and guide opens by language; do not infer from views alone. |
| Partner distribution | One useful, co-reviewed guide vs. a generic product introduction | Partner reply, repost, referral visits, and feedback quality. |

Do not chase a viral spike if it produces no guide use. Avoid mass posting links into groups, fake urgency, bought followers, engagement bait, and mass AI-generated near-duplicate pages. Google advises that search content should help people first; search-volume demand should choose questions to answer, not justify thin pages.

## Measurement

**North-star learning metric:** weekly people who reach a useful guide and report that it helped them decide a safe next step or complete a repair. Vitni currently has privacy-preserving analytics for events such as `guide_opened`, `repair_guide_completed`, and `community_help_opened`; it does **not** confirm that a physical repair succeeded. Treat those as activation proxies until you have a voluntary outcome question.

Review every Monday:

1. **Distribution:** reach, 3-second hold/watch-through, full views, saves, shares, profile visits, and link clicks by platform.
2. **Activation:** guide opens and completed repair requests from the social campaign links; compare English/French and bike/home/clothes at an aggregate level.
3. **Search:** Search Console queries and pages, impressions, clicks, and CTR. Improve high-impression pages that don’t earn clicks; expand only when the guide adds safety context, clear steps, and a useful illustration.
4. **Community:** helpful replies, partner conversations, partner-shared links, and repair outcomes offered voluntarily.

Use one tagged URL per channel/campaign (for example, `utm_source=instagram&utm_medium=organic_social&utm_campaign=chain_guide`). Keep repair descriptions and photos out of analytics, spreadsheets, and public examples unless a person has explicitly consented. GA4 remains opt-in as already configured.

### Practical 90-day output goals

These are controllable work goals rather than reach forecasts:

- **12 original repair videos**, each adapted for Reels, TikTok, and Shorts.
- **12 matching carousels or photo posts**, reused from the same repair research.
- **12 useful guide updates or newly verified guides**, with English and French coverage where quality review is possible.
- **30 thoughtful community replies** and **30 individually researched local partner contacts** over the quarter.
- **At least 10 voluntary user feedback notes** about where a guide helped or confused someone.

If producing at this rate makes the repair guidance rushed or unverified, reduce volume and keep the quality bar. The compounding asset is a trustworthy repair answer people save and send to someone else.

## Free discovery tools

- **Google Search Console:** review actual queries, pages, impressions, clicks, and CTR once vitni.cc is verified. Use real demand and page performance to decide what to improve. [Search Console Performance report](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/17010961?hl=en)
- **TikTok Creator Search Insights:** look for searched topics and content gaps inside TikTok; use these as hypotheses to test with useful repairs. [TikTok announcement](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/creator-search-insights?lang=en)
- **Instagram Professional Dashboard:** check its Best Practices guidance and account-specific tips rather than relying on static “algorithm hacks.” [Instagram Best Practices](https://about.fb.com/news/2024/10/best-practices-education-hub-creators-instagram/)

Search discoverability should follow Google’s people-first guidance: first-hand useful context, accurate instructions, clear authorship/review where appropriate, and no mass production just to target query lists. [Google Search Central: helpful, reliable, people-first content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content)
